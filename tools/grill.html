Round 1 — product

Everything below depends on these three. If they change, most of Round 2 changes with them.

01

What is this, described to someone who isn't you?

The code already answers this implicitly — no auth, no storage management, no paper-out recovery, a hardcoded "Thermal Camera" footer on every print. That's a personal object, not a product for strangers. I want it stated out loud, because it decides what we're allowed to skip.

My recommendation: a novelty/art instant camera for you and friends. It matches what's built, and committing to it lets you delete whole categories of work (jam recovery, unattended boot, storage limits) with a clear conscience.
02

Who physically operates it, and can they be told how?

"Strangers, unattended" is a different machine. It needs paper-out detection wired up (has_paper() exists in the driver and is never called), jam recovery, and a boot that survives someone yanking the power mid-print.

My recommendation: you and friends, with one sentence of explanation. Unattended-stranger mode is a v2 conversation, and it roughly doubles the failure handling.
03

What powers it — on the plywood rig, and after?

The printer pulls roughly 1.5–2 A peaks while the head is burning, against a Zero 2W's ~0.35 A idle. This is the constraint most likely to produce weird, intermittent, wrongly-blamed-on-software faults — brownouts look like corrupted prints.

My recommendation: a wall adapter (5 V, 3 A+) on the plywood rig, then a USB power bank for the first portable build. An internal LiPo needs a charger board, protection circuitry and a fire-safety opinion — that's a sub-project, and it's not the interesting one.

Round 2 — what survives the Zero 2W

A Zero 2W is roughly 5× slower per core with 512 MB of RAM. Something has to give; these decide what.

04

The live e-ink preview loop — does it survive?

This is the single most expensive thing in the codebase. _capture_loop spins with no sleep, grabbing 1640×1232 RGB, LANCZOS-resizing and Floyd–Steinberg dithering in Python — then throws away almost every frame, because the queue is maxsize=1 and e-ink can't keep up. What survives is already ~1.5 s stale by the time you see it.

My recommendation: capture on button press only. You are paying a full core, and most of your RAM budget, for a laggy preview that arrives too late to compose with. Killing it removes the frame-dropper, the capture thread, and most of the Zero 2W CPU problem in one edit.
05

If live preview goes, what is the e-ink actually for?

Depends on 04. A panel that only shows a still image after the fact is doing a much smaller job — worth checking it still earns its place, its cost, and its GPIO.

My recommendation: last captured photo plus a one-line status. Confirming the shot worked is the only job preview was really doing; framing was never realistic at 1.5 s of lag.
06

Capture resolution?

The printer is 384 px wide. You currently capture 1640×1232 — about 4× the print width in each axis — and throw the detail away in a LANCZOS downscale. On a 512 MB board, those intermediates are not free.

My recommendation: drop to ~640×480. Still comfortably above print width, so dithering has real data to work with, but a fraction of the RAM and resize cost. Measure on actual hardware before locking it in — this is a guess until a Zero 2W says otherwise.
07

The Zero 2W serial port conflict — how do we handle it?

The printer is on /dev/ttyAMA0 at 9600. On a Zero 2W the PL011 UART behind ttyAMA0 is claimed by Bluetooth by default. The fallback, mini-UART ttyS0, has a baud rate that drifts with CPU frequency scaling. Your original bitmap corruption bug was a baud mismatch — a drifting baud rate would bring it back intermittently, which is far worse than reliably.

My recommendation: dtoverlay=disable-bt in /boot/config.txt and keep ttyAMA0. You are not using Bluetooth. Do not accept a UART whose baud rate depends on the governor.

Round 3 — the browser emulator

Your mid-session ask: test the preview and menu with no camera and no hardware.

08

Where does the dithering run in the emulator?

This is the only real design decision in the emulator, and it's about trust. If the browser dithers differently from the device, the emulator will quietly lie to you about what the panel shows — and an emulator you can't trust is worse than none, because you'll design against it anyway.

My recommendation: POST the image to tools/serve.py and let the real PIL pipeline return the bitmap. The server already exists. One dither implementation means the emulator physically cannot drift from the device. Cost: you need Python and Pillow on the dev machine, which you already do.
09

What does the emulator need to cover?

Both output targets are small and fully specified — 176×264 and 384 px, both 1-bit — so the emulator can be pixel-exact rather than an approximation. The question is scope.

My recommendation: everything except simulated timing at first — then add timing early and honestly. A menu that feels fine at instant speed can be unusable at 1.5 s per refresh, and that's exactly the mistake an emulator should catch rather than cause.
10

What does the menu button actually do?

GPIO 6 has been reserved across five commits, lib/int/ui.py is zero bytes, and the callback logs and returns. Either it has a job you can name in a sentence, or it doesn't need to exist yet. A menu framework for a four-button device with a 1.5 s screen is a classic weekend that ships nothing.

My recommendation: reprint last photo, plus safe shutdown. Those are the two things you'll genuinely want while holding the object. Heat and contrast tuning belongs in config.py where you can sweep values, not behind four buttons and a slow screen.

Round 4 — process

Sequencing. Cheap to get right now, expensive to get wrong later.

11

Plywood rig: bench fixture, or v1 enclosure?

These have opposite requirements. A fixture wants every wire exposed and every board on standoffs you can unscrew. An enclosure wants things hidden and tidy. Trying to be both produces something that's awkward to probe and doesn't look good.

My recommendation: bench fixture, explicitly ugly. If it has to look presentable you'll stop probing it, and probing it is the entire reason it exists.
12

When do we actually port to the Zero 2W?

Porting a moving target means debugging the port and the features simultaneously, and you can never tell which one broke the print.

My recommendation: after the capture→print path is frozen. Freeze it on the Pi 5, where iteration is fast, then port once against a known-good behaviour you can diff against.
